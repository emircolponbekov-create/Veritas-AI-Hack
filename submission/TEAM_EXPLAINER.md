# Explain the project without pretending to know more than the data

## One sentence
“We use intake temperature and the season to estimate a completed month's cooling-system temperature difference, then check whether that estimate is better than two train-only median rules.”

## Follow one record
A record belongs to **one plant ID and one cooling-system ID**, for example Surry / 3806 / CW1. A plant can contain several systems. The target is ΔT = monthly mean discharge temperature − monthly mean intake temperature, in °C. It is not a downstream thermometer reading or the change of the whole river.

The adapter reads a frozen original CSV or selects exact IDs from local Parquet. Raw means are °F: C=(F−32)×5/9, and a temperature difference in °F is multiplied by 5/9. It checks IDs, months, missing values, duplicate months, conversion arithmetic and source methods. We do not infer units from the size of a number.

Eligibility requires a complete finite temperature pair and an explicitly continuous or interval thermometer method. Text “False” is false. Estimated, permitted and unknown methods are ineligible. Suspect measured rows are retained and flagged: having eligible=true does not override review_required. No missing target is interpolated, and no record is dropped because a model later makes a large error.

## Why the split is by time
Train is 2010–2021, validation is 2022–2023 and test is 2024–2025. We never shuffle. Train learns coefficients, scaling and medians. Validation selects alpha from 0.1, 1, 10, 100. The selection is saved before the final test evaluation, with no refit on validation/test. Each system has its own model and number of usable months; Donald C Cook systems have 12 test months, Surry has 22, most evaluated systems have 24.

## Inputs and Ridge
The only inputs are `intake_temperature_c`, `month_sin=sin(2πm/12)` and `month_cos=cos(2πm/12)`. The two calendar numbers describe season smoothly across December and January. Calendar month is known in advance; the monthly intake mean is known only after the month and public reporting may arrive later. Therefore “retrospective estimate” is accurate; “advance forecast” is not.

StandardScaler subtracts train means and divides by train scales. Ridge is a linear regression with a penalty on large coefficients; alpha controls that penalty. It is understandable and appropriate to a small monthly dataset. We do not claim that this simple form captures plant operations or river physics.

## Two comparisons everyone should understand
Overall median predicts the same train ΔT median every test month. Seasonal median predicts the train median for that calendar month. Neither looks at validation or test targets to choose its values. The better baseline is the one with the smaller MAE in the reported comparison; we do not retrain or redesign the model after seeing it.

MAE = mean(|estimate − measured ΔT|). Its unit is °C, and lower is better. For estimates with errors 1, 2 and 3°C, MAE is 2°C. In the real release, all three methods use exactly the same eligible test months within each system. These repeated monthly observations are not independent randomized trials.

## Actual findings to remember
17 systems are evaluated, spanning 14 sites. Ridge beats the best baseline in 4 cases and is worse in 13. Surry CW1: Ridge 1.849°C versus best baseline 1.995°C (22 months). Calvert Cliffs SYS042: Ridge 0.392°C versus best baseline 0.301°C (24 months). Full results, including every weak result, are in RESULTS_AUDIT.md. These counts are descriptive; no confidence interval or statistical significance was established.

December 2025 Surry: measured ΔT 6.667°C, estimated 11.029°C, absolute error 4.362°C. Intake/season do not represent every operational factor; that is a plausible limitation, not a verified explanation of that month.

## Leakage: the shortcut that would invalidate the result
Including discharge temperature plus intake would reveal the answer by subtraction. Including current ΔT or any derivative of it also leaks the target. Fitting a scaler or medians on all years leaks future information. Trying many designs after inspecting test makes test part of model selection. The saved code uses a whitelist, train-only transforms, validation selection and fixed chronological boundaries to avoid these shortcuts. File checks support this protocol; they cannot independently reconstruct every human decision made before the logs were saved.

## What the UI and notebook actually do
The UI reads a registry and verified saved exports. It does not train. Refresh rereads files without a cache; wrong hashes/identities, missing files or a partial export withhold model metrics. review_required values are not shown as confirmed measurements. Measurements and estimates are labelled separately.

RESULTS_REVIEW.ipynb loads and verifies existing artifacts; visible outputs were generated by executing that review locally. It contains no fit. The original Surry notebook contains training code but no saved outputs; do not run it for this submission pass, since its local export path is `data/` and it could overwrite legacy outputs. An artifact review is not evidence that training was performed in Colab.

## Boundaries and fair use
These data cover a local U.S. snapshot, not all plants worldwide. Monthly system ΔT does not describe peaks, downstream mixing, organisms, human health, or safety. Surry is not a model for Akkuyu. The project does not estimate avoided CO₂, a causal climate effect, or a full fuel life cycle. Residents can use it to ask better questions and examine evidence, not to decide that a site is safe or unsafe.
