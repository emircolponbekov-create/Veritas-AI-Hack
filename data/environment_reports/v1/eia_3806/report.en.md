# Surry

Evidence period: Water: 2010–2025; fish: 1970s studies summarized in 2001; radiation sample: March–April 2024; general climate review: 2014. These are historical, different periods.

Historical records document warmer cooling-water discharge and fish caught by the water intake. They do not establish an overall environmental benefit, harm or safety verdict, or describe today’s conditions.

## Water

In the reviewed 2010–2025 monthly record, water leaving cooling system CW1 was warmer than water entering it. This confirms heating across that system, not the temperature change of the whole James River.
State monitoring in 2024 also recorded tritium, a radioactive form of hydrogen, in a discharge-canal water sample. This is a separate finding from temperature; it is not by itself evidence of illness or ecological harm.
The reviewed collection does not establish current river-wide heating or chemical damage. A laboratory nondetection would not mean that a substance is entirely absent.

## Animals and plants

EPA summarized 1970s Surry studies in which fish were caught against intake screens and most survived. That documents a physical pressure and historical fish-return performance, not proof that the intake was harmless.
For land animals, plants and habitats, this reviewed collection lacks enough field evidence to establish a positive or negative effect of the station. Historical fish findings must not be presented as current counts.
The operator also reported no detectable local fish-population impact in older studies. That addresses a different outcome: it does not mean that no individual organisms were caught or lost.

## People

Insufficient evidence for this station to draw a conclusion about effects on health or living conditions. No suitable exposure history, health-outcome study or independently checked local-benefit assessment has been reviewed here. Environmental sampling alone cannot answer those questions.

## Climate

General context only: a 2014 IPCC review reports lower greenhouse-gas emissions over the full nuclear electricity supply chain than for coal or gas. This possible climate advantage is not a measurement of Surry’s emissions or emissions avoided.
No station-specific climate benefit or harm is established by this report. User-entered emissions scenarios elsewhere in the project are illustrations, not measured Surry outcomes.

Source: [IPCC 2014 electricity life-cycle review, Table A.III.2](https://www.ipcc.ch/site/assets/uploads/2018/02/ipcc_wg3_ar5_annex-iii.pdf) — Electricity supply-chain comparison, table on page 1335.

## What remains unknown

We still need current intake surveys, receiving-water measurements, habitat studies, chemical tests and appropriate exposure assessments. The evidence does not support a single environmental score. Missing observations and a failure to detect harm must never be read as proof of safety.
